# 18 · Name options

Strategist, 2026-10-26. Why we're looking: "Stumble" collides with Stumble Guys (Scopely, owns the word on Play), old StumbleUpon "STUMBLE!" marks, and a 2023 US "STUMBLE" Class 9 filing (doc 16).

**Caveat:** these checks come from web searches only. They are not a formal USPTO/EUIPO/WIPO search, and web search does not see the full Play Store. Run TSDR, EUIPO eSearch and a live Play/App Store search on the finalist before publishing.

## 1. Longlist (30)

- **Coined:** Cratefall, Blindcrate, Digwell, Spinnow, Platterly, Groovyard
- **Record shop / vinyl:** Dead Wax, White Label, Test Pressing, Locked Groove, Run-Out, Inner Sleeve, Bargain Bin, Cut-Out Bin, Listening Booth, Crate Digger, B-Side, Flipside, Needle Drop
- **Blind listening:** Blindfold, Plain Sleeve, Unsleeved, No Sleeve, Blindspin, Mystery Spin, Unlabeled
- **Discovery / serendipity:** Deep Cut, Hidden Track, Detour, Rabbit Hole

Dropped without a search: **Needle Drop** (Anthony Fantano's "The Needle Drop" is a big music brand), **B-Side / Flipside / Rabbit Hole** (crowded, many apps), and **Bargain Bin / Cut-Out Bin** (they sound cheap).

## 3. Recommendation

**Top 3**

1. **Cratefall**: coined, nothing else uses it, and it keeps the "stumble into music" idea while saying Crate Dig. It's the easiest to trademark and get domains for.
   *Play title:* `Cratefall: Music Discovery` (26 chars)
2. **Plain Sleeve**: the best metaphor for Blind Test, and it fits the record-shop look (a stamp on a blank sleeve is a ready-made logo). The only risk is the small Mac app "Sleeve".
   *Play title:* `Plain Sleeve: Music Discovery` (29 chars)
3. **Blindcrate**: names both main features in one word, and "blind" works well in Hebrew. It's less poetic.
   *Play title:* `Blindcrate: Music Discovery` (27 chars)

**Keep "Stumble" with a descriptor**
- *Play title:* `Stumble: Blind Music Discovery` (30 chars, exactly the limit)
- Risk: **medium**. We would still sit under Stumble Guys in Play search, and the 2023 "STUMBLE" Class 9 filing could block registration or lead to a complaint if it is granted. The descriptor helps users tell the apps apart but does not fix the trademark issue. This is fine for a free side project with no trademark plans. It is a bad base if the app is ever monetised or promoted.

**Suggested next step:** pick one of the top 3, then (a) search USPTO TSDR and EUIPO in Classes 9 and 41, (b) search Play and the App Store directly, (c) check the .com/.app domains with a registrar. Only after that, rename the strings in the app (`package.json` productName, Capacitor `appName`, the window title).
